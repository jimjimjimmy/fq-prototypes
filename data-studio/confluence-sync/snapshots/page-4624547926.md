## 🎯 Objective

This initiative establishes Data Studio as a production-grade, trustworthy data platform. Today, customers have limited visibility into what's happening inside their pipelines, no proactive alerting when things go wrong, inconsistent error messaging that requires support escalation to interpret, and a publish workflow that wipes all historical data on every configuration change.

Four foundational capabilities address those gaps together:

1. **Logging & Audit** — Full visibility into pipeline run history and configuration changes, with customer-facing Logs tabs at the model, connector, and global level. Satisfies SOC 1 controls 6.1, 6.8, and 6.18.

2. **Notifications** — Proactive email alerts when jobs fail, with clear distinction between self-healing failures (no action needed) and permanent failures (action required). Admins no longer discover problems when accountants notice stale data.

3. **Error Patterns & Handling** — Consistent, plain-language, actionable error messages across Data Studio. Every error tells the admin what broke, where, and what to do next. Internal system detail never surfaces to customers.

4. **Versioning & Lifecycle** — Non-destructive publish workflow with effective dates. Configuration changes go live from the effective date forward; historical data is preserved. The current "publish and re-upload everything" pattern is eliminated.

---

## 🏅 Why This Is Important

Data Studio sits upstream of the financial close. Every pipeline that runs through it feeds the numbers that accounting teams rely on. When the platform isn't transparent, predictable, and safe to change, the risk doesn't stay in Data Studio — it flows downstream into reconciliations, close deadlines, and financial reporting.

Today, the gaps are significant:

* Admins have no reliable way to know whether their pipelines ran, failed, or what changed and when. The only signal that something went wrong is often an accountant noticing stale data at close.

* "How does FloQast notify us when an integration fails?" is now a standard question in enterprise security reviews. The current answer doesn't hold up for upmarket customers.

* Error messages are inconsistent and sometimes expose internal system states. Admins can't self-serve on failures — they open support tickets.

* Every configuration change requires wiping all historical data and re-uploading from scratch. Admins avoid making necessary corrections because the cost is too high.

These aren't isolated UX gaps — they're platform reliability and trust gaps that affect every customer operating Data Studio at close.

On the compliance side, three SOC 1 controls (6.1, 6.8, 6.18) apply to Data Studio and must be operating effectively by 2026-09-30. Without the Logging & Audit work, FloQast is exposed to audit findings.

---

## 💡 Key Benefits

* Customers have confidence their data is reliably flowing — and know immediately when it isn't

* Admins self-serve on failures before the close team notices — no support ticket required

* SOC 1 6.1, 6.8, and 6.18 compliance met by the 2026-09-30 deadline

* Every configuration change — including AI-assisted ones — is traceable to a responsible human

* Plain-language error messages tell admins what broke and what to do next, every time

* Configuration changes are safe: non-destructive publishing preserves historical data and allows bounded reprocessing

* Enterprise customers can answer "how do we know when an integration fails?" in security reviews
